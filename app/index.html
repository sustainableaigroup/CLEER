How much does AI inference use?

How to interpret this dashboard

This dashboard applies CLEER, a new approach for estimating the environmental impact of closed AI models. It combines peer-reviewed research, large-scale empirical testing, and practical application to corporate emissions inventories.

How CLEER works

CLEER (Closed-model Latent Energy Estimation Range) estimates per-token energy for models that cannot be measured directly. It benchmarks open models on known hardware, matches proprietary models to the closest proxies based on observed performance, and projects their energy use onto measured power curves. What the comparisons show

The dashboard translates per-token estimates into representative chat and agentic sessions derived from public production datasets. Every model receives the same token workload, so differences primarily reflect energy intensity rather than how verbose each model tends to be. Actual results will also depend on the number of tokens a model generates in practice.

Workload sources: ShareChat conversations, Qwen serving traces, and AgentX coding-agent sessions.

From energy to emissions

Results include accelerator and host-server energy, idle capacity, cooling and power distribution, and embodied emissions from hardware and data center construction.

Scenario shown: behind-the-meter gas generation at 640 gCO₂e/kWh, combined with US-average facility assumptions for PUE. Carbon results can vary by more than 10× across plausible electricity sources; CLEER can be applied using different energy and emissions assumptions. Emissions Calculation →
